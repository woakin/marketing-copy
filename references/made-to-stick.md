# Made to Stick (SUCCES) for Website Social Proof & Metrics
**Core Concept:** Website visitors scan quickly and forget abstract claims instantly. To convert skeptics, data, metrics, and social proof must be sticky, concrete, and credible.

---

### The SUCCES Principles for Websites

- **Simple:** Strip away complexity. If a metric or claim requires a paragraph to explain, it fails. State one core point with unmistakable clarity.
- **Unexpected:** Break the visitor's browsing autopilot. Highlight counterintuitive insights or shocking statistics that challenge common assumptions.
- **Concrete:** Ground every claim in sensory, physical terms. Replace abstract buzzwords (*"scalable architecture"*) with concrete reality (*"handles 50,000 concurrent checkouts without a hiccup"*).
- **Credible:** Use the **Sinatra Test** (*"If we can handle compliance for tier-1 banks, your startup's data is bulletproof"*). Use exact, non-rounded numbers (e.g., *"14,218 active users"* > *"10,000+ users"*).
- **Emotional:** Connect data to human relief or pride. Don't just show *"30% time saved"*; show *"Reclaim your Friday evenings with your family"*.
- **Stories:** Compact customer snapshots. A mini-story inside a testimonial card creates mental simulation for the prospective buyer.

---

### Scoring (max 6 points):
- **S (Simple)** — Core takeaway is grasped in under 3 seconds (1 pt)
- **U (Unexpected)** — Features a compelling hook or pattern-interrupting fact (1 pt)
- **C (Concrete)** — Grounded in specific, measurable realities instead of abstract claims (1 pt)
- **C (Credible)** — Backed by verifiable proof, exact numbers, or Sinatra-test references (1 pt)
- **E (Emotional)** — Ties directly to visitor identity, relief, or aspiration (1 pt)
- **S (Stories)** — Uses narrative customer proof rather than generic platitudes (1 pt)

---

### Verification Checklist

- [ ] Would a first-time visitor understand the stat or proof point without domain expertise?
- [ ] Are claims backed by concrete numbers, named customer examples, or third-party validation?
- [ ] Does the proof block evoke emotional confidence or relief?
- [ ] Are abstract metrics translated into tangible, everyday human impact?
